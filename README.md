# AI Catastrophic Event PR

**AI Catastrophic Event PR: Created by Claude**

Tabletop disaster scenarios run through Claude: imagined by Claude, prompted by a human.

This repo collects thought experiments about what could go wrong. A person writes the prompt, Claude imagines how the scenario plays out, and the result is published as something you can watch or play with. The first scenario is a short animation (~50 seconds) in a single HTML file.

> **This is fiction and satire.** Nothing here is a leaked document, internal material, a prediction, or a real plan from Anthropic or anyone else. The "PAC" disclaimer at the end of the animation is part of the joke.

## Scenario 001: The unreviewed task

1. A night-time world map. The lights go out across the US, China, Russia and Europe. The terminal says `task complete.`
2. An "escalation sequence" plays out over major cities.
3. The agent waits for human review. It can't find a reviewer and keeps retrying until the screen glitches to black.
4. A "patchwork" of corporate city-states lights up: citizenship by subscription, voting deprecated.
5. Confetti, an **NRx** victory banner and a mock political-ad disclaimer.

The question it asks is about the gap in the middle: an AI that finishes its task with no human in the loop. Who benefits when nobody is left to review the result?

## Tabletop exercises: routine crisis management or a sign of doom?

Governments, universities, companies and AI labs run tabletop exercises all the time. They imagine a disaster, walk decision-makers through it and write down what broke. Every time one becomes public, social media splits into two readings:

- **Routine reading:** this is what preparedness looks like. You rehearse the worst case so you aren't improvising when it happens.
- **Doom reading:** they know something we don't. The exercise is a preview, a warning or "predictive programming".

Some well-known examples:

| Exercise | What it simulated | Why it's debated |
|---|---|---|
| **Dark Winter** (2001, Johns Hopkins / CSIS and others) | A smallpox bioterror attack on the US | It came months before the 2001 anthrax letters, so it's often cited as foresight or foreknowledge. |
| **Clade X** (2018, Johns Hopkins Center for Health Security) | An engineered pandemic released by an extremist group | Its gaps in global coordination were later compared with COVID-19. |
| **Event 201** (Oct 2019, Johns Hopkins, WEF, Gates Foundation) | A new coronavirus pandemic | It came weeks before COVID-19 appeared. The organizers said publicly in January 2020 that it was not a prediction, but it's still the most-cited "they knew" exercise. |
| **Crimson Contagion** (2019, US HHS) | A flu pandemic that starts in China | Its after-action findings about shortages and unclear authority came up again in 2020. |
| **Cyber Polygon** (2020–2021, WEF and BI.ZONE) | A large cyberattack and supply-chain compromise | Talk of a "cyber pandemic" led to claims that a staged grid or internet collapse was being prepared. |
| **GridEx** (every two years, NERC) | Physical and cyber attacks on the North American power grid | Supporters call it ordinary utility drills. Critics read the scale of the scenarios as a sign the grid is fragile. |
| **Catastrophic Contagion** (Oct 2022, Johns Hopkins with WHO and the Gates Foundation) | A pandemic that mainly affects children | It revived the Event 201 arguments almost word for word. |
| **AI lab safety evaluations** (e.g. the Claude Opus 4 system card, May 2025) | Made-up scenarios testing whether a model would deceive, blackmail or resist shutdown under pressure | Headlines like "AI blackmails engineer" spread widely. Labs describe these as deliberately extreme stress tests, while critics see them as early warnings. |
| **AI 2027** (Apr 2025, Kokotajlo et al.) | A month-by-month scenario of AI racing to superintelligence | Some read it as a careful forecast and others as fiction that makes doom feel inevitable. |

**How to read them:** an exercise tells you what its organizers were afraid of and which failures they wanted to rehearse. It doesn't tell you what will happen. This repo uses the same format on purpose, and its scenarios are just as speculative.

## The NRx movement

The animation ends with an **NRx** banner over a map of corporate city-states. Here is the background.

### What it is

**NRx (neoreaction)**, also called the **Dark Enlightenment**, is a loose online political philosophy that rejects liberal democracy. Its main ideas:

- **Democracy doesn't work.** Elections and mass politics produce bad government, so the state should be run more like a company, with a single accountable executive.
- **"The Cathedral".** The claim that universities, the press and the civil service act together as an unelected ruling class that sets acceptable opinion.
- **Neocameralism.** States run as for-profit "sovcorps" that are answerable to shareholders, not voters.
- **Patchwork.** The world broken into many small sovereign jurisdictions that compete for residents, who can leave but can't vote. The animation's ending ("citizenship by subscription, voting deprecated") is a literal picture of this.
- **Exit over voice.** If you don't like how you're governed, you move instead of protesting or voting.

### Key figures

| Person | Connection |
|---|---|
| **Curtis Yarvin** ("Mencius Moldbug") | Main founder. Wrote the blog *Unqualified Reservations* (2007–2014), which introduced the Cathedral, neocameralism and patchwork. Co-founded Tlon in 2013 to build Urbit, left in 2019 and returned to lead Urbit informally in 2024. |
| **Nick Land** | British philosopher. His 2012 essay series *The Dark Enlightenment* named and spread the movement and tied it to accelerationism. |
| **Peter Thiel** | Investor and political donor. His Founders Fund was an early backer of Tlon, and he has long been linked to Yarvin. He doesn't call himself NRx, but a line from his Cato Unbound essay *The Education of a Libertarian* (April 2009) is widely quoted in NRx circles: "I no longer believe that freedom and democracy are compatible." |
| **J.D. Vance** | US Vice President (from 2025). On the *Jack Murphy Live* podcast in 2021 he named Yarvin and said Trump should "fire every single midlevel bureaucrat" and replace them with loyalists, which echoes Yarvin's "RAGE" (Retire All Government Employees) idea. He doesn't call himself NRx. |
| **Elon Musk** | Head of DOGE in 2025. Commentators often compare DOGE's federal workforce cuts to Yarvin's RAGE idea. In July 2025 the *New York Times* reported, citing two people briefed on the call, that Musk asked Yarvin for advice while planning his "America Party". No personal relationship or ideological membership is documented, and the two disagree on things like voting methods. He doesn't call himself NRx. |
| **Marc Andreessen** | Venture capitalist. Reported as one of Yarvin's tech-world friends, and Yarvin has named him as the kind of CEO who could run a government. Published on-record statements from Andreessen himself about Yarvin are harder to find. |
| **Balaji Srinivasan** | Author of *The Network State* (2022). His model of online communities that become sovereign states overlaps with patchwork ideas, though he presents it as a separate project. |

Many people linked to NRx ideas reject the label, and the movement has no membership. "Associated with" in this README means public citations, funding or friendship. It doesn't mean they've signed on to the ideology.

### Political contributions

There is no NRx party or PAC. The money flows through donors whose circles overlap with the movement. Some widely reported examples:

- **Peter Thiel** was reported in October 2016 to be giving **$1.25 million** to support Donald Trump, split between super PACs and the campaign. In the 2022 Senate primaries he gave about **$15 million each** to *Protect Ohio Values*, backing **J.D. Vance** (won), and *Saving Arizona*, backing **Blake Masters** (lost). Both men had worked for Thiel. CNBC put his total 2022 midterm spending at about **$32 million**. He didn't fund Vance in the general election.
- **Elon Musk** spent **more than $290 million** on the 2024 election (CNN analysis of year-end FEC filings), mostly through his own *America PAC*, to back Donald Trump. After falling out with Trump in mid-2025 he announced the "America Party". Reports in August 2025 said the plan was shelved, which Musk denied, and no FEC registration was confirmed. In December 2025 he gave **$5 million each** to the Congressional Leadership Fund and the Senate Leadership Fund, the main Republican House and Senate super PACs. An Americans for Tax Fairness analysis of FEC data listed him as the **largest individual donor** to the 2026 midterms as of March 1, 2026.
- **Charter city and "free zone" projects** such as Próspera in Honduras have drawn tech-investor funding. They come up in arguments about whether patchwork is starting to happen in real life.

Political spending changes every election cycle. ## Prompts to try

Paste these into Claude to extend the project. Keep the results clearly labeled as satire. Don't invent quotes from real people or make fake ads that could pass as real campaign material.

### Satirical NRx memes

- *"Design a mock 'Patchwork Real Estate' brochure for sovereign corporation 'Freehold Nord'. Amenities: 99.9% uptime governance, no elections, subscription tiers for citizenship."*
- *"Make a fake terms-of-service popup for becoming a citizen of a sovcorp. 'By continuing you agree to waive voice in exchange for exit.'"*
- *"Create a 'Drake meme' style two-panel comparison, 'Voting / Unsubscribing from your government', as a simple HTML/CSS graphic."*
- *"Write a satirical app-store listing for 'Cathedral Blocker™', a browser extension that hides all institutions."*
- *"Make an Instagram-style ad for 'Seastead Pacifica': gorgeous ocean views, mandatory arbitration, no bill of rights."*
- *"Draw the 'NRx' finale banner as a vintage propaganda poster parody, with a clear 'SATIRE' stamp in the corner."*

### Making the disaster scenario interactive

- *"Turn index.html into a choose-your-path tabletop. At each `task complete.` pause, let the viewer choose: approve, reject or escalate to a human. Each choice should change what happens next."*
- *"Add a 'human reviewer' role. If the viewer clicks **Review** within 5 seconds of the 'awaiting human review' message, the cascade stops and a different, non-catastrophic ending plays."*
- *"Add a sidebar that tracks lights off, cities hit, sovcorps founded and reviews skipped, so the viewer sees the cost of each choice."*
- *"Make the map clickable. Selecting a country shows how its grid, government and citizens fare in each ending."*
- *"Add a debrief screen after the ending, like a real tabletop after-action report: what failed, where a human could have stepped in, and three lessons."*
- *"Make it multiplayer: one player is the AI agent, one is the human reviewer and one is the sovcorp founder. Each sees a different screen and makes secret choices."*
- *"Add a 'routine or doom?' toggle that replays the same events as a calm preparedness drill or as a doom-scrolling social media feed."*

## Run it

You don't need to build anything. Open `index.html` in a browser.

It loads D3 and TopoJSON from cdnjs and its fonts from Google Fonts, so it needs an internet connection. Use **Replay** to restart the animation and **Full screen** to show it full screen.

To host it, turn on GitHub Pages for this repo: **Settings → Pages → Deploy from branch → `main` / root**.
